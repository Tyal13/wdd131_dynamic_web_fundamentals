    Why password strength matters

    A strong password is your first line of defense for every online account. These tips follow the password guidance from NIST, the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology.

    • Length matters most. NIST recommends a password of at least 15 characters.
    • Use a passphrase: join real words into something long and memorable, like "cassette lava baby".
    • You do not need special characters or numbers. NIST no longer requires them, though they can still help.
    • Avoid common or breached passwords such as "password" or "12345".
    • Use a unique password for every site, so one breach does not unlock everything.
    • Use a password manager, ideally one that supports multifactor authentication.
    • Turn on multifactor authentication wherever you can for an extra layer of protection.

    Is my password already compromised?

    Data breaches are common. NIST notes there were more than 3,000 breaches in 2024 (reported by the Identity Theft Resource Center), so a password you already use may be on a public list. You can check whether your email or passwords have appeared in a known breach with a free tool like Have I Been Pwned.
